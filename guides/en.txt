What is EPS-TOPIK?
EPS-TOPIK is the Korean language test for people who want to come and work in Korea under the Employment Permit System (EPS). Korean companies choose workers from the list of people who passed this test.
Who needs it?
The Employment Permit System lets people from countries that have an agreement with the Korean government work in Korea on an E-9 (non-professional employment) visa.
You need this test if you want to work in Korea in a factory (manufacturing), on a farm or with livestock, in fishing, in construction, or in some service jobs.
Check the official website to see which countries take part.
What can I do after I pass?
Passing the test does not mean you go to Korea right away. Passing is the first step.
Pass EPS-TOPIK
Health check, job application and skills test
Your name goes on the job seekers' list
A Korean company chooses workers from the list
Labor contract → E-9 visa → arrival in Korea
A higher score gives you a better chance of being selected. So it is important to get a high score, not just to pass.
What does the test look like?
There are two parts: reading and listening. There is no writing or speaking.
Every question is multiple choice.
You take the test on a computer or tablet.
Along with everyday Korean, many questions use workplace language: tools, safety and work instructions.
How do I apply?
You take the test in your own country, not in Korea.
You apply through your government's sending agency (the office in charge of overseas employment).
Test dates and job sectors are different in each country and each year.
The Employment Permit System is run directly between governments. Be careful of any person or agency (broker) who says they will send you to Korea if you pay them.
Things people often confuse
EPS-TOPIK and TOPIK have similar names, but they are different tests. Passing EPS-TOPIK does not let you enter a university or give you a TOPIK level.
Your pass is valid only for a limited time. If no company chooses you in that time, you must take the test again.
You still need Korean after you arrive. TOPIK and KIIP help if you want to work for a long time or change your visa status.

What is TOPIK (Test of Proficiency in Korean)?
TOPIK is the official Korean language test run by the Korean government (NIIED, under the Ministry of Education). It shows the Korean ability of non-native speakers in levels from 1 to 6.
Who needs it?
People who want to enter a Korean university or graduate school
People who want a Korean government scholarship
People who want a job at a Korean company or a company that works with Korea
People who want to change their visa status in Korea or earn points for it
People who want official proof of their Korean ability
What can I do with a TOPIK level?
University admission: many universities ask for level 3 or higher to enter a program taught in Korean, and a higher level to graduate. It differs by university and department.
Jobs: positions where you work in Korean usually ask for an intermediate level or higher.
Visas: in some visa reviews, your TOPIK level counts as your Korean language score.
KIIP: if you have a TOPIK level, you can start the program at a higher stage.
The level you need depends on the university, company or visa type. Always check the rules of the place you want to go.
What does the test look like?
TOPIK I (beginner, levels 1–2): listening and reading
TOPIK II (intermediate and advanced, levels 3–6): listening, reading and writing
You do not choose a level. Your total score decides it. If you take TOPIK II, you get a level from 3 to 6 according to your score.
There is a paper test (PBT) and an internet test (IBT).
The speaking test is a separate test that you apply for separately.
How do I apply?
In Korea, you apply on the official TOPIK website.
Outside Korea, you apply through the local test organizer (a Korean Education Center, embassy, university and so on).
The test is held several times a year. Registration often closes quickly, so check the schedule early.
Things people often confuse
Your score is valid for only 2 years from the day results are announced. Plan your test date around your admission or visa application.
Even a very high score on TOPIK I cannot give you level 3 or higher. If you need level 3 or higher, you must take TOPIK II.
TOPIK and EPS-TOPIK are different tests. To go to Korea to work under the Employment Permit System, you need EPS-TOPIK.
For permanent residency, completing KIIP is often required rather than a TOPIK level.

What is KIIP (Korea Immigration and Integration Program)?
KIIP is an education program run by the Ministry of Justice so that foreigners living in Korea can learn the Korean language and Korean society. Completing it helps when you apply for a visa status, permanent residency or naturalization (Korean citizenship).
Who needs it?
People who want to live in Korea long-term and change or extend their visa status
People who want permanent residency (F-5)
People who want to naturalize and get Korean citizenship
Foreigners living in Korea: marriage migrants, workers, international students, overseas Koreans and others
People who are registered as foreign residents in Korea can join. Check the official website for the exact conditions.
What are the benefits of completing it?
You can get extra points or meet the Korean language requirement when you apply for or change a visa status.
It is accepted as the basic knowledge requirement when you apply for permanent residency.
It can replace the comprehensive (written) test when you apply for naturalization.
The benefits depend on your visa status and the stage you completed. Before you apply, check with the immigration office or the official website.
How is the program organized?
There are stages 0 to 5.
Stages 0–4: Korean language and culture (basic → beginner → intermediate)
Stage 5: Understanding Korean society (basic course for permanent residency, advanced course for naturalization)
Pre-test: decides which stage fits your level
Level test: taken at the end of each stage
Mid-term test: taken after stage 4 to move up to stage 5
Comprehensive test: the final test after stage 5
How do I apply?
Sign up on the Soci-Net website (Immigration & Social Integration Network).
Apply for the pre-test and get your stage. If you know no Korean at all, you can start from stage 0. If you have a TOPIK level, you can be placed in the stage that matches it.
Choose a nearby training center and register for a class.
Things people often confuse
KIIP is not a single test. It is a course where you attend classes and pass evaluations. It takes a long time, so it is better to start early.
The evaluations have a written test (multiple-choice questions and short writing) and a speaking (oral) test.
TOPIK levels and KIIP stages are not the same. TOPIK is a test that proves your ability. KIIP is an education program connected to visa status, permanent residency and naturalization.
